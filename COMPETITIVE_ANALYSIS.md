## Executive Summary

The microdosing app market is still nascent and fragmented. Approximately 10-12 dedicated microdosing tracking apps exist, but none has achieved dominance. Most suffer from one or more of: platform limitations (iOS-only), buggy releases, abandoned development, limited free features behind paywalls, inflexible protocol scheduling, poor retroactive logging, and lack of wearable/health data integration.

The r/microdosing subreddit has grown from 27,000 subscribers (2018) to 183,000+ (2022) and continues to grow. RAND's 2025 nationally representative survey confirms microdosing is mainstream. Yet users consistently report resorting to pen-and-paper journals, spreadsheets, or general-purpose mood trackers (like Daylio) because no dedicated app fully meets their needs.

**The biggest opportunity is a privacy-first, cross-platform app that combines robust dose tracking, flexible protocol scheduling, rich journaling, customizable wellness metrics, data visualization/correlation, and wearable integration -- all without requiring cloud accounts or exposing users to legal risk.**

## App-by-App Analysis

### 1. Microdose.me / Quantified Citizen

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Developer** | Quantified Citizen Technologies Inc. (Vancouver, Canada) |
| **Platform** | iOS, Android |
| **Launched** | November 2019 |
| **Pricing** | Free |
| **Primary Purpose** | Research study participation (not personal optimization) |
| **Participants** | 33,000+ as of January 2024 |

**Key Features:**
- Part of the largest mobile microdosing study in the world, led by Paul Stamets and MAPS Canada
- Daily questionnaires (~1 minute/day) over a 3-month study period
- Cognitive performance tests and mental health assessments
- Tracks anxiety, depression, stress, ADHD, mood
- Apple Health / Google Fit integration (sleep, steps, heart rate, mindfulness minutes)
- Custom trackers: daily habits, measurements, timeline views for retroactive data entry
- Visual comparison of tracker data to explore correlations
- Fully anonymous: no name, email, or date of birth required
- Data encrypted and stored on Canadian servers

**User Reviews & Ratings:**
- Concept highly praised: "I really like the idea of what this study is trying to do"
- Gratitude video series described as "really nice"
- **Major complaints:**
  - "The app seems to have a lot of bugs and does not flow well"
  - "Doesn't allow you to fill out missed tasks"
  - Users reported being "locked out from being able to further participate for an unknown reason"
  - Duplicate notifications
  - One user spent all day entering data but found nothing to do afterward, with the study showing only 24% completion and no activities left
  - Inability to set up trackers
- Developer has been responsive, fixing bugs in v1.2.5+

**Gaps & Limitations:**
- Primarily a research tool, not a personal tracking/optimization app
- Rigid study structure -- users cannot customize their tracking experience freely
- Bug-prone with lockout issues
- Study-focused design means features serve researchers, not necessarily users
- No free-form journaling capability
- Limited personal insight generation

## Non-App Alternatives

Users who find dedicated apps insufficient often turn to:

### General-Purpose Mood Trackers
- **Daylio** -- Frequently recommended on Reddit; highly customizable mood and habit tracker with icon-based logging, visual stats, and history. No text journaling required. Users praise it as "the way to go" over Houston.
- **Day One** -- Full journaling app with cross-device sync. Offers rich text entries but no microdosing-specific features.
- **Bearable** -- Symptom tracker for pain, mood, and medication. Can be adapted for microdosing tracking.

### Notion Templates
- **Microdose Pro Notion Template** -- Custom variables, fully customizable metrics, updated for 2024. More flexible than any app.
- **General health tracking Notion templates** -- Medication/supplement trackers, mood journals, habit tracking databases.

### Spreadsheets
- Google Sheets / Excel with custom tracking columns
- Static but effective for users who want full control

### Pen and Paper
- Physical microdosing journals available on Amazon (e.g., "Microdosing Journal - Wonderland," "Microdosing Tracker" by Better Energy Publishing)
- Include guided prompts and tracking templates

## User Pain Points & Frustrations

Based on app store reviews, forum discussions, and user feedback gathered from the Psily design case study, Reddit r/microdosing analysis, and Trustpilot reviews:

### 1. Platform Limitations
- Most apps are iOS-only (Houston, Psily, MyDelica, Trip)
- Android users are underserved
- No quality cross-platform/web solution exists
- Users want to access data from phone, tablet, and computer

### 2. Inflexible Protocol Scheduling
- Houston offers only 3 preset plans with no custom option
- Deliqs locks dosage amounts per protocol (cannot vary dose day-to-day)
- Users follow diverse protocols (Fadiman, Stamets, Intuitive, custom) and need flexibility
- Users report: "I find that the perfect dose tends to vary by day"

### 3. Retroactive Logging Failures
- One of the most common complaints across all apps
- Users forget to open the app and cannot log past days
- Houston initially had no past-day logging (later fixed)
- Quantified Citizen doesn't allow filling out missed tasks

### 4. Bugs and Stability Issues
- Quantified Citizen: lockout bugs, duplicate notifications, freezing
- Psily: app goes blank after entering data
- Deliqs: empty introduction screens, unreachable buttons
- Psily: reminder notifications don't actually fire

### 5. Privacy and Legal Concerns
- Users are logging potentially illegal activity
- Concern about data being subpoenaed or discoverable
- Some apps require accounts/cloud storage (risk)
- MicroTracker's fully-offline approach is rare but valued
- GDPR, CMIA, CCPA create fragmented protections

### 6. Paywalls on Basic Features
- Deliqs locks basic functionality behind Premium
- Users complain: "Features locked behind Premium button wanting me to subscribe to basic functionality"
- Frustration when free version is too limited to evaluate the app

### 7. Abandoned Development
- Houston last updated March 2022
- MicroTracker stuck at v1.0.7
- Several apps show no recent activity

### 8. Research Tool vs. Personal Tool Mismatch
- Quantified Citizen/Microdose.me serves researchers, not users
- Rigid study structure doesn't serve personal optimization needs
- Users want personal insights, not just to contribute to studies

### 9. Limited Data Visualization Options
- Users want multiple chart types (splines, bars, or no graphs at all)
- One-size-fits-all visualization doesn't work
- Users want to see their "big picture" trend over weeks/months

### 10. No Wearable/Health Data Integration
- Only Quantified Citizen has Apple Health integration
- Psily has wearables in beta
- No app correlates microdose data with sleep quality, HRV, steps automatically
- Users must manually combine data from separate apps

## Most Requested Features

Synthesized from app store reviews, the Psily design case study (which drew from Reddit r/microdosing and mental health forums), community discussions, and user feedback forms:

### Tier 1: Critical (Most Frequently Requested)
1. **Custom protocol scheduling** -- ability to create any schedule, not just presets
2. **Retroactive logging** -- log past days easily when forgotten
3. **Cross-platform availability** -- iOS AND Android AND web
4. **Privacy-first architecture** -- offline-first, no mandatory accounts, encrypted local data
5. **Flexible dose tracking** -- vary dose amounts day-to-day, track multiple substances

### Tier 2: High Priority
6. **Rich journaling** -- free-form text tied to daily check-ins
7. **Customizable wellness metrics** -- choose what to track, favorite important metrics
8. **Multiple visualization types** -- bars, lines, trends, or no graphs; user choice
9. **Stack tracking** -- log combinations (psilocybin + Lion's Mane + niacin, etc.)
10. **Data export** -- PDF reports, CSV, shareable with therapist or doctor

### Tier 3: Differentiators
11. **Wearable integration** -- Apple Health, Google Fit, Oura, Whoop for sleep/HRV/steps
12. **Correlation analysis** -- automatically show relationships between doses and outcomes
13. **AI-powered insights** -- predict dips in well-being, suggest activities
14. **Substance-agnostic design** -- support psilocybin, LSD, MDMA, cannabis, supplements
15. **Multi-language support** -- international user base
16. **Community features** -- optional, privacy-respecting sharing of anonymized insights
17. **Integration period tracking** -- track on-days AND off-days equally
18. **Baseline assessment** -- measure state before starting protocol for comparison
19. **Intention setting and reflection** -- guided prompts for meaningful journaling
20. **Apple Health medication tracking integration** -- leverage iOS built-in medication features

## Market Gaps & Opportunities

### Gap 1: No Quality Cross-Platform Solution
Every app is either iOS-only, Android-only, or web-only. No single app covers all three platforms well. A React Native (Expo) app can serve iOS, Android, and web from a single TypeScript codebase.

### Gap 2: No True Privacy-First App with Rich Features
MicroTracker is privacy-first but feature-poor. Feature-rich apps (Deliqs, Houston) require accounts or cloud storage. There is no app that combines rich tracking, journaling, visualization, AND fully local/encrypted data storage.

### Gap 3: No Stack Tracking
The Stamets Stack (psilocybin + Lion's Mane + niacin) is the most popular microdosing protocol, yet only Psily offers any form of "stack" tracking. Over 50% of psilocybin microdosers combine psilocybin with other substances, but no app makes this easy to track.

### Gap 4: No Wearable Correlation
Users want to see how their microdosing correlates with objective health metrics (sleep quality, HRV, activity). Only Quantified Citizen does this, and only for research purposes. No personal tracking app offers automatic correlation between doses and wearable health data.

### Gap 5: Dosing Flexibility
Users report that their optimal dose varies day-to-day. Deliqs locks dosage per protocol. Houston has rigid presets. Users need the ability to log any dose amount on any day.

### Gap 6: Integration Period Tracking
Most apps focus on dose days but don't equally track integration/off days, which are when many effects are noticed. Users need robust tracking of both dose and non-dose days.

### Gap 7: Abandoned Apps Create User Distrust
Houston (last updated 2022) and MicroTracker (stuck at v1.0.7) create skepticism. Users want apps with active, ongoing development.

### Gap 8: Apple App Store Gatekeeping
Apple has been cautious about psychedelic-related apps (forced Deliqs to change its mushroom icon). A web-based PWA approach sidesteps App Store review entirely, avoiding content policy risks.

### Gap 9: Substance-Agnostic Design
Most apps are implicitly or explicitly about psilocybin. Cannabis microdosing (growing rapidly in 2025) and other substances (LSD, MDMA, ketamine) are underserved.

### Gap 10: Therapist/Professional Integration
MicroTracker can export PDF reports, but no app has a proper therapist-sharing workflow. Users increasingly work with integration coaches and therapists who want to see tracking data.

## Privacy & Legal Landscape

### Key Privacy Risks
- Mental health app data is classified as "special category" under GDPR
- Logging illegal substance use creates discoverable records
- Personal information may be available to US government under legal process
- Many apps have ambiguous privacy policies regarding third-party data sharing
- Even "anonymized" data can potentially be re-identified

### Best Practices (Rare in Current Apps)
- **MicroTracker:** No cloud, no login, no APIs. Data never leaves device.
- **Microdose.me:** Fully anonymous; no name, email, or DOB. Encrypted Canadian servers.
- **Psily:** Anonymous account creation, no PII required.

### Legal Considerations
- Psychedelics remain controlled/illegal in most jurisdictions
- Apps must avoid promoting illegal activity while serving users
- Apple App Store review scrutinizes psychedelic-related content
- A web-based approach avoids app store gatekeeping entirely
- Local-only storage minimizes legal exposure for users
